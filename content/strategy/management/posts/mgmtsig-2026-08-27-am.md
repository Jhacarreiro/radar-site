1️⃣ **Design the conversation before the meeting**
HBR studied more than 1,000 participants across 100 teams. Teams that left discussions with clear owners and next steps were roughly four times more likely to be rated effective.

💡 Why it matters
Define the product of the meeting, the decision-maker and how disagreement will surface. An agenda lists topics; it does not tell the room what must be settled.

☕ Coffee talk
Who owns the decision in your next executive meeting, before the discussion gets polite?

---

2️⃣ **Treat other people's time as an operating asset**
Stanford's Huggy Rao separates bad friction, which exhausts people, from good friction, which slows overconfidence and forces reflection.

💡 Why it matters
Remove approvals, recurring meetings and handoffs that consume attention without improving the outcome. Add a deliberate pause only where a rushed call is costly.

☕ Coffee talk
Which recurring meeting survives because the organiser never pays the time bill?

---

3️⃣ **Name one decision-maker for every major call**
Checkr COO Lindsey Scrase says the company assigns a single decision-maker to each major decision, even as its C-suite meets daily without a fixed agenda.

💡 Why it matters
Fast access to the executive team helps surface issues. Named decision rights stop that access from turning every call into collective ownership.

☕ Coffee talk
Which decision is still waiting because six executives are all "aligned" and none is accountable?
