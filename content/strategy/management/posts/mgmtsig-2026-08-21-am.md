1️⃣ **Test readiness before you announce the strategy**
HBR's advice is simple: assess whether the team is ready before rolling out a new strategy. A launch date is not evidence that the organization can execute it.

💡 Why it matters
Make readiness explicit: ownership, capability, capacity and the trade-offs people must accept. Fix the gaps before the town hall, not after the first missed milestone.

☕ Coffee talk
Which part of your new strategy depends on a team that is already overloaded?

---

2️⃣ **Keep judgment upstream of the AI answer**
HBR warns that AI can weaken leaders' judgment. The risk starts when a polished answer replaces the work of framing the question and testing the assumptions.

💡 Why it matters
Use AI to widen options and challenge a view. Keep the decision criteria, dissent and final call with named people who can explain what they rejected and why.

☕ Coffee talk
Who in the room can still say the model's neat answer is wrong?

---

3️⃣ **Give each agent a narrow job**
Claire Vo's week with Grok Bot points to a practical design choice: separate agents for product, commitments, invoices and case studies, each connected to the accounts it needs.

💡 Why it matters
One general assistant creates vague ownership and messy permissions. A small roster of role-based agents makes scope, access and performance easier to inspect.

☕ Coffee talk
Would you hire one person to chase invoices, manage product and monitor every inbox?
