1️⃣ **Retain people by removing the reason to move**
MIT Sloan argues that retention works better as a system of mobility barriers than as a sequence of counteroffers. Pay is only one part of why valuable people stay.

💡 Why it matters
Map what each critical employee would lose by leaving: relationships, autonomy, learning, status or a credible next role. Then manage those ties before an offer arrives.

☕ Coffee talk
What would your hardest-to-replace person actually miss if a rival doubled the offer?

---

2️⃣ **Do not let civility suppress the real argument**
HBR warns that an excessively nice culture can make people soften criticism, delay conflict and leave weak decisions unchallenged.

💡 Why it matters
Psychological safety should protect candour, not comfort. Leaders need to reward useful dissent and make disagreement normal before the meeting ends.

☕ Coffee talk
Which decision sailed through your last executive meeting because everyone was being pleasant?

---

3️⃣ **Manage the relationships inside the boardroom**
INSEAD's board research says governance quality depends on how directors relate to one another, not only on their individual credentials and formal independence.

💡 Why it matters
A board can have the right skills and still miss the issue if status, alliances or deference constrain the discussion. Chairing is relational work.

☕ Coffee talk
Who changes the subject in your boardroom without ever asking to?
