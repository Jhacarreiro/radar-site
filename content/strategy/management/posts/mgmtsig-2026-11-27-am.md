1️⃣ **Decide where the hours go before rolling out AI**
BCG surveyed 6,349 employees across nine Asia-Pacific markets. Only 20% had strong clarity about their employer's AI strategy, including what to do with the time they saved.

💡 Why it matters
Pick one queue or backlog that should shrink after rollout. Give its owner the time back explicitly, then check whether the queue moved. A tally of hours saved won't tell you that.

☕ Coffee talk
Whose backlog gets smaller when the first team saves an hour?

---

2️⃣ **Take work out before taking a day off**
In a six-month study covering 141 organisations, teams reorganised work before trying a shorter week at the same pay. Employees reported less burnout. The comparison group did not show the same pattern. Employers volunteered for the trial.

💡 Why it matters
Before piloting a shorter week, find something the team can stop doing. Check service levels and after-hours work alongside burnout; a cleaner calendar can hide work pushed into evenings.

☕ Coffee talk
Which Friday task would we actually drop, and who would notice?

---

3️⃣ **Give novices and veterans separate AI scorecards**
A study of 5,172 customer support agents found that AI assistance lifted issues resolved per hour by 15% on average. Less experienced agents improved on speed and quality. The most experienced saw slight speed gains and a small drop in quality.

💡 Why it matters
Run the pilot by tenure and review answer quality separately. Otherwise the aggregate can make a useful training aid look like a good default for the people handling escalations.

☕ Coffee talk
Would your most experienced agents be allowed to turn the suggestions off?
