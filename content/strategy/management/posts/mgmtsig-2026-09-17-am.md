1️⃣ **A seat in the meeting is not a veto**
Bain's RAPID framework separates input from approval and final authority. Approval roles should be rare, reserved for constraints such as legal or regulatory requirements.

💡 Why it matters
For the next blocked decision, name the decider and distinguish required approval from advice. Assign execution too.

☕ Coffee talk
Who in your last decision meeting had quietly awarded themselves a veto?

---

2️⃣ **Agree the hiring scorecard before meeting the candidate**
Structured interviews use common job-related questions and scoring criteria. Research supports their predictive value; interviewers should score answers independently before the panel discussion.

💡 Why it matters
Set the evidence for a strong answer before interviews begin. Keep the most senior person's verdict out of the initial scoring.

☕ Coffee talk
Could your panel explain the winning candidate's score without saying they just felt like a better fit?

---

3️⃣ **A quiet team may be protecting itself**
Psychological safety means people can raise concerns without humiliation. It does not remove performance standards; fear of looking incompetent can keep useful information out of the room.

💡 Why it matters
Ask for concerns about a live project and examine your response to the first one. Keep the performance standard explicit.

☕ Coffee talk
Does the person who flags a project risk leave your meeting wishing they had stayed quiet?
