1️⃣ **A management job is an expensive sales bonus**
Research on sales promotions found that firms favoured current sales performance over indicators of managerial ability. Strong individual results did not reliably identify the best future managers.

💡 Why it matters
Reward sales results without making management the only route up. Look for evidence of improving other people's work.

☕ Coffee talk
Does your top seller want to run the team, or is that just the only promotion you offer?

---

2️⃣ **Ask for advice you can use next time**
Experiments on developmental input found that asking for advice, rather than feedback, elicited more concrete suggestions. They did not establish that recipients' performance improved.

💡 Why it matters
For development conversations, ask what to change next time. Keep that separate from rating past performance.

☕ Coffee talk
How many comments in your last review told you what to do differently on Monday?

---

3️⃣ **The boss's joke has a captive audience**
INSEAD's September essay warns that laughter around a boss is a poor test of humor. Experimental research finds inappropriate jokes can reduce perceived competence and status.

💡 Why it matters
Avoid making a direct report the punchline. Laughter at the all-hands may reflect deference to your position.

☕ Coffee talk
Would that all-hands joke survive without your job title attached?
