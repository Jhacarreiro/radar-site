1️⃣ **Hybrid work can make teamwork invisible**
In 38 group interviews with roughly 760 employees at one financial-services firm, researchers found a shift toward individual tasks and away from informal help. The qualitative study describes weaker ties and harder onboarding; it does not show that hybrid work fails everywhere.

💡 Why it matters
If scorecards reward only individual output, mentoring and peer help become unpaid work. Put those contributions into expectations, and make office days useful for collaboration.

☕ Coffee talk
If helping a new hire is invisible in the KPI, who keeps doing it?

---

2️⃣ **Leadership training needs a place to land**
A September study proposes four supports for leadership learning to stick: diagnosis, feedback, chances to apply it and shared ownership. Its survey covered 238 senior HR-development professionals in DACH multinationals; it is exploratory, not a causal test.

💡 Why it matters
Before buying another course, name the work where the new behaviour will be used, who will give feedback and which manager owns follow-up.

☕ Coffee talk
Who owns the change once the course calendar ends?

---

3️⃣ **Indirect instructions trade clarity for autonomy**
Across six studies, indirect leader communication sometimes raised employees' sense of autonomy and task performance. It also lowered perceived leader effectiveness and increased withdrawal. The mixed result is the point.

💡 Why it matters
State the outcome and boundaries clearly; leave room on how to get there. Ambiguity is a poor substitute for a decision the manager has not made.

☕ Coffee talk
Can people choose the method without guessing what you actually want?
