1️⃣ **AI design quietly changes who gets to decide**
INSEAD separates AI that helps managers direct and monitor work from tools that let colleagues coordinate directly. One strengthens vertical control; the other can move coordination sideways.

💡 Why it matters
Choosing an AI tool is also an org-design choice. Decide whether it adds managerial oversight or removes handoffs.

☕ Coffee talk
If the agent routes the work and flags every exception, who still gets to overrule it?

---

2️⃣ **Anxiety can turn a transformation into theatre**
A four-year study of a professional-services firm found that unmanaged anxiety can push leaders and staff into defensive routines: blame and activity that leave the status quo intact.

💡 Why it matters
More change plans will not fix a response that protects people from discomfort. Leaders need to make room for the anxiety before asking teams to absorb another change.

☕ Coffee talk
When the steering group starts pointing fingers, is the problem still the plan?

---

3️⃣ **Run two tests before backing one**
Jay Parikh says he would rather test two competing approaches than forecast a winner. A six-case study found simultaneous experiments can preserve strategic variety and long-term survival, but slow early growth.

💡 Why it matters
Parallel tests buy evidence, not certainty. Keep both small and agree what result will end or combine them.

☕ Coffee talk
If both bets stay funded, what will force the choice?
